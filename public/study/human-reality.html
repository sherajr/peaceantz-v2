The Human Reality

What is the human being capable of becoming—and how can the light received by one soul contribute to the unity and betterment of the world? Enter a six-unit study rooted in the Bahá’í writings.

How to use this study individually or with a group
  • Suggested rhythm: study one unit per gathering, allowing roughly 60–90 minutes. Depth matters more than completing every question.
  • Collective study: begin with prayer, read each quotation aloud, allow silence, hear every willing voice, and choose a modest act of service before the next gathering.
  • Learning through action: begin the following gathering by reflecting on what happened, what was learned, whose perspective was absent, and what should change.
  • Invitational atmosphere: no prior Bahá’í knowledge is assumed, and no participant should feel pressured to disclose private experiences or speak before they are ready.
About this material: This is an independently prepared supplementary study aid, not an official Bahá’í or institute course. The quotations carry the authority of their respective sources; the explanatory text, diagrams, questions, and biographical reflections represent the author’s understanding and are offered for study and consultation.
Pilot-group reflection guide

After each gathering, record brief observations rather than trying to judge whether participants gave “correct” answers:

  • Which passage generated genuine insight, prayerful attention, or purposeful consultation?
  • Which explanation, question, visual, or term was confusing—or seemed to carry more authority than it should?
  • Did the pace allow silence and participation, or did the group feel pressure to finish?
  • Did the proposed action arise from a real relationship and expressed need? Was it completed?
  • Whose voice was missing, and what could make the next gathering more hospitable?
Unit One

The Noble Reality of the Human Being

Purpose: to recognize every person as a noble spiritual reality endowed with powers that can know, love, choose, serve, and reflect the attributes of God.

ONE
Integration

One noble reality.
Many powers.

We distinguish the soul’s powers not to construct a mechanical diagram of the person, but to recognize the capacities entrusted to every human being and the divine qualities they can reflect.

Unit Two

The Soul in This World

Purpose: to explore the soul’s relationship with body, heart, moral orientation, and choice without reducing the person to matter or imagining several competing inner selves.

Unit Three

Receiving Divine Light

Purpose: to encounter Revelation, nearness to God, prayer, and meditation as powers that awaken, orient, and illuminate the human soul.

Unit Four

Freedom and Spiritual Growth

Purpose: to understand how love, divine law, will, effort, confirmation, tests, reflection, repentance, and renewed action transform capacity into character.

ARISE
Learning through action

Guidance is encountered
on the path of effort.

Pray. Study. Choose. Act. Reflect. Repair. Try again. Confirmation does not remove human responsibility; it meets sincere souls as they arise and learn.

Unit Five

Becoming a Source of Light

Purpose: to develop spiritual qualities through service, consultation, relationships, hospitality, and the patient work of building unity in diversity.

Unit Six

Advancing Civilization

Purpose: to connect the transformation of the soul with justice, the harmony of science and religion, spiritual and material progress, and sustained contribution to the betterment of the world.

BECOME
The recurring movement

Revelation becomes life
through a cycle.

Study. Prayer. Meditation. Choice. Action. Tests. Reflection. Repentance. Confirmation. Better action. The cycle repeats—and character slowly takes form.

The mirror and the Sun

Six units.
One movement.

Recognize the mirror. Turn it toward the Sun. Let the light it receives become love, unity, justice, and service in the world.

What kind of soul am I becoming? Who is being warmed by the light I have received? What will I do now?
